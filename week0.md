## 2480

All maps lie because they simplify the world to make it understandable for us. Cartographic conventions help us navigate those lies.
Examples: we know which projections work best for different purposes/parts of the world, we know which data classification schemes are appropriate for different goals

## 3480

Every policy or business decision is the product of choices about what to map and how. Domain expertise, professional ethics, and reflection help us navigate those choices.
Examples: knowing which model to pick to transform data for a suitability analysis, being transparent about alternative analyses or limitations of datasets

80% of datasets are spatial, so most decisions involve spatial data. When we make decisions, we are making spatial decisions. How?

_What other kinds of spatial decisions exist?_

# Decisions

A decision is the selection of a specific course of action from among a set of decision alternatives or options.

One distinction (we'll review more next week): **formal** (e.g. where to put a factory) vs **informal** (e.g. which restaurant to go to) decisions

# Science of teaching and learning (SOTL)

- Importance of reflection
  - reflection is a bridge between mechanical execution and deep conceptual understanding
  - SOTL research shows students don't recognize the skills they have! Software proficiency vs spatial thinking, critical data evaluation, and formal communication
  - metacognition (awareness of how you think/learn)
- Spatial and technical problem-solving
  - autonomy and resilience
  - error as failure vs diagnostic info
> *"GIS taught me two invaluable skills that I use every day.  The first one is problem-solving. Working with GIS often means troubleshooting technical issues and finding creative solutions for project deliverables. I learned to leverage the ArcGIS help pages and community resources to overcome challenges."* - Aidan Hawkins
- The limitations of AI
  - GeoAI
  - AI tries to smooth things out for us, but friction is inherent to the learning process
